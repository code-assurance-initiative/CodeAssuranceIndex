# Decision 0011: Adopt an interim pragmatic rule in place of the standstill

- **Decision:** 0011
- **Date:** 2026-11-06
- **Decided by:** Jimmy Borch, for the CAI team (the acting maintainer, and today the whole team, until the steering
  group exists: [GOVERNANCE.md](../GOVERNANCE.md))
- **Proposal:** [0011: An interim pragmatic rule, so a clear improvement is not frozen until a steering group exists](../proposals/0011-interim-pragmatic-governance.md)
- **Outcome:** Accepted as written, its two questions answered below.
- **Supersedes:** nothing. It replaces the standstill paragraphs of GOVERNANCE.md, which were description, not a prior
  decision.

## Conflicts and recusals

The proposer and the decider are the same person, who also maintains the CCA engine measured by the benchmark. This
decision changes the procedure under which that person decides every other proposal, so the conflict is general, not
tied to one dimension. Nobody else can decide today, so nobody recused. The safeguard against the conflict is not a
second vote that does not exist; it is that this rule keeps every decision recorded, reversible, and open to an
unaffiliated objection after adoption, and keeps the mechanical guarantees (immutable versioned rubrics, no secret
scoring, a licence to fork) that do not depend on the decider behaving well.

## Comment

None was taken before adoption, and under the rule this decision adopts none is required before adoption. The 14-day
post-adoption period (this change moves no number) runs to **2026-11-20**; an unresolved objection from a commenter
not affiliated with the proposer reopens the decision. This bootstrap — a procedure accepted under its own terms — is
recorded plainly, as decisions 0001 and 0002 recorded their departures from the then-current process.

## Reasons

- The standstill assumed the steering group was near. It is not: no founding date exists, and the honest wait is
  one to five years. A standard frozen that long stops raising quality, which is the only reason it exists.
- The page already disagreed with the practice. Six decisions were taken by one maintainer, and 0006 ran its comment
  period after adoption. GOVERNANCE.md says the page, not the practice, is what is wrong when they differ.
- Freezing is not the protection it looks like. The protection against a conflicted decider is the record, the
  reversibility and the mechanical guarantees — all of which this rule keeps — not the absence of any decision.
- Reversibility is real here in a way it is not for most standards: a rubric version is immutable and the
  specification is CC BY, so an interim decision that the steering group later dislikes can be replaced, and anyone
  who disagrees in the meantime can fork rather than be bound.

## Answers to the proposal's questions

1. **Any unresolved unaffiliated objection reopens the decision**, not only a sustained one. "Unresolved" means the
   decider could not answer it to the commenter's satisfaction; the reopening is itself a numbered follow-up decision
   that states what the objection was and what changed. The bar is deliberately low, because the post-adoption period
   is the only place an outside voice enters while there is one decider.
2. **The interim rule carries a standing review.** At each year's turn the acting maintainer re-states in
   GOVERNANCE.md whether it still applies and why the steering group does not yet exist, so the interim state cannot
   quietly become permanent by inattention.

## What did not yet

- **The steering group still does not exist**, and bar 1 and bar 4 of GOVERNANCE.md remain unmet in substance. This
  decision makes the interim state honest and workable; it does not end it. Finding one unaffiliated reviewer who can
  answer the post-adoption comment remains the first real step toward the group.
